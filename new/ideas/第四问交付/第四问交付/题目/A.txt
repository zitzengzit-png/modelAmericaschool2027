Problem A: Modeling Ebola Virus Disease Transmission and Control



Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) is a highly fatal infectious disease caused by the Ebola virus, primarily transmitted through direct contact with the bodily fluids of infected or deceased individuals. Since its first identification in Zaire (now the Democratic Republic of the Congo) in 1976, Africa has experienced more than 25 Ebola outbreaks. The 2014–2016 West African outbreak was the largest in history, with over 28,000 reported cases and more than 11,000 deaths.[1]

In May 2026, the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) experienced another Ebola outbreak. As of October 25–26, 2026, the outbreak has persisted for approximately five months and has become the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record (second only to the 2014–2016 West African outbreak), as well as the largest and deadliest Ebola outbreak in DRC's history.[2]

This outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, which differs from the commonly seen Zaire strain. Currently, there are no approved vaccines or specific treatments for the Bundibugyo virus. The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) in May 2026.[3]

As of October 25–26, 2026, there have been approximately 10,060 cumulative confirmed cases; approximately 4,887 cumulative deaths; a crude case fatality rate of approximately 48.6%; the outbreak has spread to 8 provinces and 66 health zones, with Ituri, North Kivu, and Haut-Uele being the most affected provinces;[4] cross-border transmission has also occurred: Kenya has reported 9 confirmed cases; [5] Nigeria confirmed its first case on October 20 (with a history of travel to Uganda).[5]

Numerous challenges remain, including: transmission chains are not effectively controlled, with over 80% of new cases detected outside known contacts; approximately two-thirds of deaths occur in the community, with the deceased not having received medical care in health facilities. Armed conflict persists in Ituri, North Kivu, and other areas, hindering outbreak response, and treatment centers have been attacked. Cross-border transmission risk exists, as the outbreak has spread to neighboring countries, with imported cases in Kenya and Nigeria. Funding is severely insufficient, with the joint WHO–Africa CDC response plan still facing a funding gap of over 400 million US dollars. There are no approved vaccines or specific drugs, as vaccine and treatment options for the Bundibugyo virus remain in clinical trials.

Simultaneously, response progress includes: treatment beds have exceeded 800; laboratory capacity has expanded from 1 to 16; contact follow-up rates have increased to nearly 80%; more than 21,000 community health workers are being trained; safe and dignified burials have significantly improved; and multiple clinical trials have been launched, including the ChAdOx1 vaccine safety trial, MBP134 and remdesivir treatment trials, and the obeldesivir post-exposure prophylaxis trial.

Glossary

Ebola Virus Disease (EVD): A severe, often fatal illness in humans caused by the Ebola virus, transmitted through direct contact with bodily fluids.
Bundibugyo virus: A species of Ebola virus first identified in Uganda in 2007, associated with case fatality rates of approximately 25–40%.
Basic Reproduction Number (R0): The average number of secondary infections produced by one infected individual in a completely susceptible population.
Effective Reproduction Number (R(t)): The average number of secondary infections produced by one infected individual at time t, accounting for existing immunity and interventions.
Case Fatality Rate (CFR):  The proportion of confirmed cases that result in death.
Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): Treatment administered after exposure to a pathogen to prevent disease onset.
Ring Vaccination: A strategy of vaccinating close contacts and contacts-of-contacts of confirmed cases to create a protective ring around each case.
